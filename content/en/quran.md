The Qur’an is the word of God revealed to the heart of Muhammad ﷺ, preserved until the Day of Judgement. The Sunnah explains it: clarifying the ambiguous, specifying the general, and showing the Prophet’s way in worship and conduct.

:::Ayah text="إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَزَّلْنَا الذِّكْرَ وَإِنَّا لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ" ref="الحجر — ٩" tr="We have sent down the Reminder, and We will guard it." note="حفظُ الله للقرآن سببُ الثقة في النص."
:::

:::Section title="Rules of recitation and practice"
:::Cards cols="3"
:::Card icon="🔊" title="Tajwid"
The science of articulation and its rules — keeping the tongue from error in the book of God. One letter can change the meaning, so mastery of recitation begins with mastery of articulation.
:::
:::Card icon="📅" title="A daily portion"
A simple plan: two pages after each prayer, or five pages after dawn. Consistency beats volume every time.
:::
:::Card icon="🧭" title="Reading for intent"
Start with the short, instructional surahs, then the legal Medinan ones, and always gather the ayat of a subject rather than quoting one alone.
:::
:::

:::Section title="What the Qur’an did for civilization"
:::Cards cols="2"
:::Card icon="🔤" title="The Arabic language"
The finest Arabic text ever written: it fixed the grammar, widened the meanings, and gave writers a standard of eloquence.
:::
:::Card icon="🧠" title="Science & method"
Verses calling to reflection, contemplation and inference — “Will they not contemplate the heavens and the earth?” — putting understanding before memorising.
:::
:::Card icon="⚖️" title="Social justice"
Limits protecting life, wealth and honour, and a standing refusal of oppression: “And let not the hatred of a people prevent you from being just.”
:::
:::Card icon="🤝" title="Human dignity"
Human worth is inherent; violating it is condemned — and the clearest Quranic stance is: “Indeed, Allah orders justice and good conduct.”
:::

:::Cards cols="2"

A mass-transmitted report, a solitary one, a disconnected one, a defective one — with the science of narrators as part of the method.

:::Card icon="🗂️" title="The collections"
The six canonical books, then the muwattas and encyclopaedic works — all read through the rules of isnad.

:::Section title='Key takeaways'
:::Cards cols='3'
:::Card icon='1' title='Start with what you love'
Open on the short surahs you already hear in your prayer and master them first — affection opens the door to consistency long before a heavy plan does.
:::
:::Card icon='2' title='Never judge one verse alone'
When a verse is hard, do not rush to a verdict: go to the Sunnah that explains it and to the scholars of tafsir, then decide.
:::
:::Card icon='3' title='A chain protects the report'
Something attributed to the Prophet ﷺ without a carefully examined chain is not something to build on — checking first beats sharing fast.
:::
:::
:::

:::Section title='Live it this week'
:::Timeline
:::Event when='Day 1' title='Set your portion'
Write down a plan you can actually keep: one page after each prayer, or five pages after dawn — and fix the mushaf, the place and the time.
:::
:::Event when='Days 2 to 6' title='Read, then write one line'
Keep the portion each day and write in the margin of your notebook one sentence you learned — even if it is a single word.
:::
:::Event when='Day 7' title='Review the week'
Count the pages you read, re-read what you wrote down, then simplify the plan or double it according to what you found.
:::

:::Section title='Quick questions'
:::FAQ
:::QA q='Is it fine to recite from my phone or laptop?'
The proper way is to hold a mushaf; if that is not possible, or you are travelling, or a device suits the place, reading from it makes recitation easy and does not get in the way — the thing that matters is that the portion does not stop.
:::
:::QA q='Do I need a teacher to correct my recitation?'
A teacher is worth having even for an hour a week: a mistake in the ear cannot always be corrected by yourself, and listening to a reciter who has it right, then repeating after him, is the best help there is.
:::
:::QA q='How do I memorise the Qur’an when I am busy?'
Take one verse a day and repeat it on your way — memorising grows from steady recitation, not from a long plan, and the small thing you keep beats the big thing you postpone.
:::
