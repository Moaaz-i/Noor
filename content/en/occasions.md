An occasion in Islam is a reminder, not a party to be replayed: a calendar for the heart, giving the soul set times for rest and for account. The Islamic year carries a month in which the doors of harm close, a night worth a thousand months, days in which good deeds are multiplied, and a weekly Friday. Whoever arranges their worship against this calendar never lets the days pass empty.

:::Section title="Six appointments in the year"
:::Cards cols="3"
:::Card icon="🌙" title="Ramadan"
The month in which fasting was prescribed and the Qur’an was sent down as guidance for people: a month of prayer, charity and remembrance, in which good deeds are multiplied and the doors of good are opened.
:::
:::Card icon="🌟" title="Laylat al-Qadr"
“The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months” (al-Qadr 3); it is sought in the last ten nights with faith and hope, and keeping it awake is worth a lifetime of worship.
:::
:::Card icon="🎉" title="Eid al-Fitr"
The first of Shawwal: the Eid prayer and its takbir, zakat al-fitr given before the prayer, then a day of greeting, kinship and rejoicing in what God has permitted.
:::
:::Card icon="🕋" title="Eid al-Adha and Arafah"
The ten of Dhu al-Hijjah and the day of Arafah: standing, supplication and repentance — then the day of sacrifice, remembering the first messenger.
:::
:::Card icon="🕌" title="Friday"
The best day on which the sun rises, containing an hour in which prayer is answered, with the reading of Surat al-Kahf as a recommended practice.
:::
:::Card icon="📿" title="The ten of Dhu al-Hijjah"
The best of days with God; fasting the day of Arafah expiates the year before it and the year after it (Sahih Muslim).
:::

:::Timeline
:::Event when="Muharram" title="The year begins"
The first of the sacred months, and the month in which the hijri dating starts; fasting the day of Ashura is recommended, recalling the day God saved Moses and his people.
:::
:::Event when="Rajab" title="One of the sacred months"
A sacred month that prepares the way into Ramadan: the Muslim increases in good and avoids wrong, and its voluntary worship is open-ended rather than fixed to a set portion.
:::
:::Event when="Sha‘ban" title="Getting ready for Ramadan"
The month of preparation — more fasting, more Qur’an, more remembrance — so that the heart and the body enter the month already disposed for it.
:::
:::Event when="Ramadan" title="The month of the Qur’an and fasting"
The prescribed duty that stands all year: fasting from dawn to sunset, night prayer, charity, and a night worth a thousand months.
:::
:::Event when="Shawwal" title="Eid al-Fitr"
Its first day is Eid al-Fitr, and fasting its six days is recommended by most scholars as a completion of Ramadan’s fasting.
:::
:::Event when="Dhu al-Hijjah" title="The days of sacrifice"
Its ten days are the best of the year, then the day of Arafah, then the day of sacrifice: performing the pilgrimage and remembering Ibrahim’s willingness.
:::

:::Section title="Three questions about occasions"
:::FAQ
:::QA q="What about voluntary fasting, such as Mondays and Thursdays?"
It is supererogatory rather than obligatory, and the Prophet ﷺ used to fast them, loving that his deeds be presented while he was fasting, since deeds are presented on those two days (Sahih Muslim). Whoever cannot join them both loses nothing: what is obligatory is not made heavier without a text.
:::
:::QA q="How should the ten days and Arafah be kept?"
Fasting the ten of Dhu al-Hijjah is recommended for one who is not standing at Arafah: “Fasting the day of Arafah expiates the year before it and the year after it” (Sahih Muslim). Whoever is present at the standing leaves the fast because of travel and standing; the purpose is nearness, not strain.
:::
:::QA q="What about celebrating something that is not an established practice?"
Established practices are bounded by the text: “Whoever does an action that is not part of our matter, it is rejected” (Sahih al-Bukhari). Ordinary customs — greetings, new clothes, visiting family — carry no sin; the criticism falls on inventing into the religion, not on rejoicing in what is permitted.
:::
