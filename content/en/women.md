A woman in Islam is a party to the covenant rather than an exception from it: her dignity is not a favour granted or a figure to be weighed, but a settled right. Her situation is therefore not measured by a passing phrase, but by texts that stand firm, that set the two parties equal at the origin, and that order rights alongside duties rather than by proportion alone.

:::Ayah text="وَالْمُؤْمِنُونَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتُ بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَاءُ بَعْضٍ" ref="التوبة — ٧١" tr="The believers, men and women, are allies of one another." note="Two parties in one pledge — not two ranks of dignity."
:::

:::Section title="Six settled rights"
:::Cards cols="3"
:::Card icon="📚" title="The right to education"
Seeking knowledge is a duty, and a woman shares in it like anyone else: “My Lord, increase me in knowledge” (Ta-Ha 114), and whoever walks a path seeking knowledge, God makes the path to Paradise easy for him (Sahih Muslim).
:::
:::Card icon="💰" title="Property and inheritance"
She owns, sells, gives away and sets conditions, and her wealth cannot be taken from her by force; she has a share in inheritance stated plainly in the Book of God, and upkeep and housing are hers by right.
:::
:::Card icon="💍" title="Choosing a spouse"
“Do not prevent them from marrying their former husbands, when they have agreed among themselves in a suitable manner” (al-Baqara 232) — agreement is the condition, and blocking without cause is forbidden.
:::
:::Card icon="🏡" title="Motherhood and family"
God exhorted the human being toward his parents: “his mother carried him, weakness upon weakness” (Luqman 14); care and tenderness are written into the relation, and leadership in the household is tenderness rather than dominance.
:::
:::Card icon="🛡️" title="Protection and fairness"
Upkeep, housing and honour fall on the husband, and fairness is commanded: “and live with them in kindness” (al-Nisa 19); the Prophet ﷺ said: “The best of you are those who are best to their families” (al-Tirmidhi).
:::
:::Card icon="🤝" title="Taking part in good"
Khadijah, may God be pleased with her, gave support, wealth and counsel; Asmaʾ carried provisions on the migration; women gave their pledge; and zakat and charity are their right as it is the poor’s.
:::
:::
:::

:::Section title="Rights, not favours"
The question is not how many rights are written down but whether the system balances: upkeep on one side, inheritance on another, and education, choice, protection and participation all around. Whoever compares the two shares without looking at the obligations has taken half the truth and left the rest. The rule is that what the text settles is not cancelled by opinion, and what changes in custom is revisited in the particulars rather than in the principles.

:::Ayah text="لِلرِّجَالِ نَصِيبٌ مِّمَّا تَرَكَ الْوَالِدَانِ وَالْأَقْرَبُونَ وَلِلنِّسَاءِ نَصِيبٌ مِّمَّا تَرَكَ الْوَالِدَانِ وَالْأَقْرَبُونَ مِمَّا قَلَّ مِنْهُ أَوْ كَثُرَ" ref="النساء — ٧" tr="For men a share of what the parents and close relatives leave, and for women a share of what they leave, be it little or much." note="Inheritance rests on kinship and giving, not on being male."
:::
:::

:::Section title="Four common questions"
:::FAQ
:::QA q="Does inheritance shortchange women?"
The text is explicit: “Allah instructs you concerning your children: for the male, what is equal to the share of two females” (al-Nisa 11). The fair comparison looks at the whole set of obligations: upkeep and housing fall on the man, the dower belongs to the woman, and there is no upkeep owed by her in return. Whoever takes the two shares out of that context has taken half the picture.
:::
:::QA q="Is a woman allowed to seek knowledge?"
Knowledge is a duty on Muslim men and women alike, in the words of the Prophet ﷺ: “Whoever walks a path seeking knowledge, God will make easy for him a path to Paradise” (Sahih Muslim). Seeking it is a condition of the well-being of the individual, the family and society, and no one is exempted on grounds of gender.
:::
:::QA q="Is the hijab optional or obligatory?"
The legal ruling is an obligation on whoever is accountable: “and draw their coverings over their chests” (al-Nur 31). How it is applied, though, is by wisdom rather than by force, since the religion itself rejects compulsion: “there is no compulsion in religion” (al-Baqara 256) — so it is invited to with gentle words and example, not with beating.
:::
:::QA q="May a woman work?"
Yes, with two conditions: that it does not conflict with her duties and that it does not harm her family; what she earns is hers to keep and to give. The seerah also records the respected working woman: Khadijah, may God be pleased with her, a merchant whose clients trusted her — Islam never barred women from earning, but made it lawful and honourable.
:::
