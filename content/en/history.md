Islamic history is not a list of names but a chain of causes: spread by invitation, flourishing by knowledge, decline by neglect. Whoever reads the causes sees the remedy.

:::Timeline
:::Event when="632–661 CE" title="The Rightly Guided Caliphate"
Abu Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthman and ʿAli: unification of the peninsula, the opening of Persia, the Levant and Egypt, and the compilation of one codex under ʿUthman.
:::
:::Event when="661–750 CE" title="The Umayyads"
Damascus as the capital, expansion into al-Andalus (711), and an administrative system with a mint and a currency.
:::
:::Event when="750–1258 CE" title="The Abbasids and the House of Wisdom"
Baghdad driving translation, mathematics, medicine and astronomy — with Ibn al-Haytham, al-Razi and Ibn Sina at their peak.
:::
:::Event when="1000–1300 CE" title="The great Islamic states"
The Seljuks and Mamluks in the east, al-Andalus and Granada in the west: architecture, agriculture and philosophy.
:::
:::Event when="1258 CE" title="The fall of Baghdad"
A political ending, not a civilisational one: scholarship moved to Cairo, Damascus, Istanbul and al-Andalus.
:::
:::Event when="1453–1924 CE" title="The Ottomans"
Istanbul as an Islamic capital for centuries, custodianship of the two holy mosques, organisation of the pilgrimage and resistance to European expansion.
:::
:::Event when="19th–21st c." title="Colonialism, then awakening"
The colonial experience, the reform movements, and the return of educational and missionary activity on every continent.
:::
:::

:::Section title="What made the golden age golden?"
:::Cards cols="3"
:::Card icon="🏫" title="Knowledge as work"
Ancient institutions (al-Zaytunah, al-Qarawiyyin, al-Azhar) were continuous teaching bodies, not static monuments.
:::
:::Card icon="🏦" title="The endowment (waqf)"
A non-profit funding model: schools, hospitals and water fountains that survived centuries without a state budget.
:::
:::Card icon="🌍" title="Open trade"
The silk road and the sea routes made markets a meeting point of civilisations, and the Islamic currency globally accepted.
:::
:::

:::Section title="A present-day reading"
:::Cards cols="2"
:::Card icon="🔎" title="Do not copy the past"
The lesson is not imitation but causes: building institutions, freedom of inquiry, and the ethics of the producer.
:::
:::Card icon="🌱" title="Start where you are"
Any civilisational experience begins with a group that learns, then an institution that serves, then an effect that spreads.

:::Section title='Key takeaways'
:::Cards cols='3'
:::Card icon='⏳' title='Knowledge outlives the state' tag='1'
Baghdad fell in 1258 and the learning moved to Cairo, Damascus and Istanbul: a civilisation does not die with its capital.
:::
:::Card icon='🗣️' title='Translation is a civil act' tag='2'
The House of Wisdom carried what others had written and then added to it: whoever learns from other nations becomes their root, not their follower.
:::
:::Card icon='🧾' title='Critique keeps a culture alive' tag='3'
Cultures did not flourish because they never erred, but because they reviewed and repaired; decline begins where criticism is silenced.
:::
:::

:::Section title='Quick questions'
:::FAQ
:::QA q='Why do history books differ in the details?'
They draw on different sources, so the stronger report is chosen through its chain and its circumstances, and disagreement is shown rather than hidden — while the main line of events stays the same.
:::
:::QA q='Did the golden age end with the fall of Baghdad?'
The fall was political, not scholarly: learning moved on to Cairo, Damascus and Istanbul, and schools and academies continued. An age ends when the questioning stops, not when a city falls.
:::
:::QA q='How do I remember dates without memorising lists?'
Chain each event: cause, then event, then effect. 1258 stays with the fall of Baghdad and 711 with the entry into al-Andalus — a number holds when it carries a meaning.
:::
