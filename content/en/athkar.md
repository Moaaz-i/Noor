Remembrance is the heart turning back to its Lord: through it the soul grows calm, the steps grow firm and fear grows quiet. God prescribed a remembrance in every prayer, and a reported remembrance for morning and evening — after the prayer, at sleep and waking, and in distress and worry.

:::Ayah text='أَلَا بِذِكْرِ اللَّهِ تَطْمَئِنُّ الْقُلُوبُ' ref='الرعد — ٢٨' tr='Calm waits on remembrance: whoever leaves it is unsettled.' note='الذكرُ رجوعُ القلب، والطمأنينةُ ثمرتُه.'
:::

:::Section title='Remembrances of day and night'
:::Cards cols='3'
:::Card icon='🌅' title='Morning' kicker='From dawn to forenoon'
“We have entered the morning and the dominion belongs to God,” “I am pleased with God as Lord, with Islam as religion and with Muhammad as Prophet,” and the chapter of sincerity and the two protectors three times. Say it after fajr; if you were rushed or busy, come back to it before mid-morning.
:::
:::Card icon='🌙' title='Evening' kicker='After the asr prayer'
“We have entered the evening and the dominion belongs to God,” “By You we enter the evening and by You we enter the morning, by You we live and by You we die, and to You is the return,” and “O God, I ask You well-being in this world and the next.”
:::
:::Card icon='😴' title='Before sleep' kicker='The last thing you say'
“In Your name, O God, I die and I live,” the last part of the chapter of the Cow, and “Glory be to God 33, praise be to God 33, God is the Greatest 34” — with breathing into the palms and wiping whatever the hands reach.
:::
:::Card icon='🍽️' title='Waking and eating' kicker='On the blessing'
On waking: “Praise be to God who gave us life after He caused us to die, and to Him is the resurrection.” At the table: “In the name of God,” and if you forgot, “In the name of God at its beginning and its end,” and at the end, “Praise be to God who fed me this and provided it for me.”
:::
:::Card icon='😔' title='Worry and grief' kicker='In distress'
“O God, You are my Lord, there is no god but You; You created me and I am Your servant,” and in distress “There is no god but God, the Mighty, the Forbearing,” and “There is no god but You; glory be to You — I have been among the wrongdoers.”
:::
:::Card icon='🔄' title='Forgiveness and repentance' kicker='A door that stays open'
“I seek the forgiveness of God, the Mighty, there is no god but Him, the Living, the Everlasting, and I turn to Him in repentance,” and “My Lord, forgive me and accept my repentance — You are the Accepter of repentance, the Merciful.” The door of repentance stays open while there is life left in you.
:::
:::

:::Section title='How to make remembrance stick in your day'
Remembrance is not measured by the number of words but by the steadiness of the heart. The road is to give every remembrance a fixed place in your day, to begin with the little you are sure of, and to understand what you are saying, line by line.

:::Hadith text='كَلِمَتَانِ خَفِيفَتَانِ عَلَى اللِّسَانِ، ثَقِيلَتَانِ فِي الْمِيزَانِ، حَبِيبَتَانِ إِلَى الرَّحْمَنِ: سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ وَبِحَمْدِهِ، سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ الْعَظِيمِ' ref='Sahih al-Bukhari & Muslim' tr='“Two words are light on the tongue, heavy in the scales and beloved to the Most Merciful: Glory be to God and praise be to Him, glory be to God, the Almighty.”' note='كلمتان خفيفتان... فما بالك بمن جعلهما عادةً ثابتةً في يومه وليلته.'
:::

:::Cards cols='3'
:::Card icon='🕐' title='A fixed place' kicker='Tie it to a time'
Say it after every prayer, before sleep and at the first light of morning. What is fixed to a time stays; what is postponed is forgotten.
:::
:::Card icon='📿' title='Little, but constant' kicker='No gaps'
Five times after each prayer, or ten after fajr: take something small you can perfect, then grow it. The little that stays is more loved than a great deal that stops.
:::
:::Card icon='🤲' title='A present heart' kicker='Understand what you say'
Read one line of meaning, then repeat it three times slowly — the tongue recites while the heart understands. Presence in the words matters more than a high count.
:::

:::Section title='Quick questions'
:::FAQ
:::QA q='Should non-Arabs say the adhkar in Arabic?'
Remembrance aims at the heart and the meaning, and God hears every language. Whoever has not memorised the Arabic may say it in their own language with the correct meaning, and learning the original Arabic wording is recommended when it becomes easy; where a wording is fixed, keeping it unchanged is the completion of the act.
:::
:::QA q='What is the best remembrance?'
The best of it is “There is no god but God,” alone with no partner; then “Glory be to God and praise be to Him” and “Glory be to God, the Almighty” as in the hadith just quoted, then seeking forgiveness and turning back to God. Whatever comes after that is extra favour and reward.
:::
:::QA q='What is the ruling on counting on the prayer beads?'
It is lawful and recommended by the majority of scholars when the intention is to keep the count without rushing the tongue — and it never replaces remembrance of the heart. The fuller act joins the two: if the meaning is lost in a high count, the point has been missed.
:::
