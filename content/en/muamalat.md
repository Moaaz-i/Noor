For Muslims the economy is both an act of worship and a goal: wealth is a trust, and profit is lawful when the intention is sound and the means are clean. Two rules open this whole chapter — growth is wanted, harm is refused. What follows is a short account of its main rulings in daily life.

:::Hadith text='الْبَيِّعَانِ بِالْخَيَارِ مَا لَمْ يَتَفَرَّقَا' ref='Sahih al-Bukhari & Muslim' tr='“The two parties to a sale have the option until they part.”' note='Freedom of choice before the parties separate; if they were truthful, a blessing rests on their sale — the basis of every contract.'
:::

:::Section title='Rulings of dealing'
:::Cards cols='3'
:::Card icon='📉' title='The prohibition of usury' kicker='The root of the ban'
An interest taken on a loan wears the debtor down and holds production back. When like is exchanged for like and one of the two is deferred, that is usury; the sound alternative is a real transfer of ownership first, then a sale at a known deferred price.
:::
:::Card icon='🚫' title='Cheating and defects' kicker='A lawful margin'
Do not sell what you do not own, do not hide a defect in the goods, and do not gloss over what lowers them. The Prophet ﷺ forbade a present person selling on behalf of an absent one — a lawful margin lies in full disclosure, never in concealment.
:::
:::Card icon='📝' title='Contracts and conditions' kicker='By intent, not wording alone'
A contract is read by its intent: offer, acceptance and a known object. A condition that serves both sides stands, and one that contradicts the text or the purpose falls — “the people are but by their intentions.”
:::
:::Card icon='💼' title='Wages and work' kicker='A right before the sweat dries'
The wage is what was agreed, not what the labour turned out to cost afterwards, and the worker is paid before his sweat dries. Whoever ruins another’s property without right owes compensation.
:::
:::Card icon='🤲' title='Borrowing and surety' kicker='A debt to be repaid'
A loan is help, not growth: whoever lends a good loan should have it returned at a known time. “When you conclude a loan, write it down” — writing prevents dispute, and no one is pressed for a debt until he is able.
:::
:::Card icon='🏦' title='Charity and repaying debt' kicker='Good that accumulates'
Charity turns misfortune away and purifies wealth, and repaying debt is among the closest acts of devotion. Whoever carries a debt should start with a clear plan for paying it, then give charity if the means allow.
:::
:::
:::

:::Section title='From unlawful to lawful: three rules'
No one needs a ruling for every single deal — only a scale that repeats: a question before the contract, detail inside it, and fulfilment after it. The wider rule of this chapter is that no harm is done and no harm is tolerated, and nothing is taken without right.

:::Ayah text='يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَذَرُوا مَا بَقِيَ مِنَ الرِّبَا إِن كُنتُم مُّؤْمِنِينَ' ref='البقرة — ٢٧٨' tr='“Leave what remains of usury” — the rest is left as the first was, and the command rests on God-consciousness rather than on calculation alone.' note='الباقي كما تُرك الأصل: وأصلُ هذا الخطاب التقوى قبل الحساب.'
:::

:::Cards cols='3'
:::Card icon='📋' title='Before the contract' kicker='Three questions'
Does this deal harm anyone else? Can I keep the promise I am about to make? Are the conditions written down and understood? If one answer stalls, do not proceed.
:::
:::Card icon='✍️' title='Inside the contract' kicker='Disclosure before sale'
Set out the item, the price, the term and the penalties in writing, and mention any defect. When a dispute comes, the document speaks first — not memory.
:::
:::Card icon='🕋' title='After the contract' kicker='Fulfilment and charity'
Give the other party what is theirs before it is due, and begin with the first of your profit: a charity that breaks your grip on the money and protects the growth that follows.
:::
:::

:::Section title='Quick questions'
:::FAQ
:::QA q='Is buying by instalments allowed?'
Yes, if the item is known and its price is known both cash and deferred, and the instalments and any penalties are written down — that is a known price, not interest. If the scheme changes the price after the contract, or extends the term by raising the instalment purely for delay, that is usury and does not stand.
:::
:::QA q='Is working in an interest-based bank allowed?'
It scales with the wrongdoing. Work that neither handles nor assists usury is a lighter question for many scholars, while lending, collecting and fixing the terms of interest are the act itself. If you see no alternative, take counsel with people of knowledge, and move quickly toward work that is lawful.
:::
:::QA q='What about work on commission or through fraud?'
Commission is fine when the work is lawful, the contract is clear and the price is known — no commission on what is forbidden, and none on goods the seller does not own. Fraud, taking property through trickery, or selling what you hold no title to is flatly unlawful; the measure is that both sides are content and each delivers what was agreed.
:::
