Worship is the pillar of the whole religion: through it a servant draws near to his Lord, and through it he puts right his own life, his family and his society. The road begins with purity, because prayer is not accepted until both the outward and the inward person are clean. What follows is practical: how to purify yourself, how to pray, and how to keep devotion alive hour by hour.

:::Hadith text='طُهُورٌ شَطْرُ الإِيمَانِ' ref='Sahih Muslim' tr='“Purity is half of faith.”' note='Half of faith: without it nothing before it is accepted, and with it everything after it finds its place.'
:::

:::Section title='Purity, step by step'
:::Cards cols='3'
:::Card icon='💧' title='Wudu' kicker='The base'
Three washings of the hands, face and arms, one wiping of the head, and washing the feet to the ankles, in a fixed order, rinsing the mouth and nose and cleaning between the toes. It starts with intention and it is undone by deep sleep, relieving oneself or touching the private parts.
:::
:::Card icon='🚿' title='Ghusl' kicker='For each major state'
Recommended after intimacy, for women in their monthly and post-natal bleeding, and before the Friday prayer. The intention sets it apart from wudu: water over the hair to its roots three times, then over the whole body once.
:::
:::Card icon='🏜️' title='Tayammum' kicker='When water is missing'
One strike for the face and one for the hands, wiping over the head and the feet. It stands in for water when water is gone, cannot be used, or would cause harm — the case the verse itself names: “and you find no water, then turn to clean earth.”
:::
:::Card icon='🕐' title='Prayer times' kicker='Five appointments'
Fajr from its dawn until sunrise, zuhr once the sun declines, asr after it, maghrib until the red glow fades, and isha until mid-night. Each time leaves room for a moderate delay, never for slipping into the time that follows.
:::
:::Card icon='🕌' title='The congregation' kicker='Twenty-seven degrees'
“Praying in congregation outweighs praying alone by twenty-seven degrees,” and the imam stands as a guarantor for those behind him: if he prays a rak‘ah, they complete it with him.
:::
:::Card icon='🕯️' title='The sunnah rak‘ahs' kicker='Twelve in all'
Two before fajr, four before zuhr and two after, two after maghrib and two after isha. Whoever keeps them, a house is built for them in Paradise.
:::
:::
:::

:::Section title='How is the prayer performed?'
Prayer is not a repetition of words but a standing between a servant and his Lord: opening with the takbir, then recitation, bowing and prostration in order, then the testification and the salam. Yet what is asked of it is larger than movement — which is why Islam cared for both its awe and its constancy.

:::Ayah text='إِنَّ الصَّلَاةَ كَانَتْ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ كِتَابًا مَوْقُوتًا' ref='النساء — ١٠٣' tr='“Prescribed at fixed times” — prayer is an appointment, not a habit to postpone.' note='ولذلك جعل الإسلام لها مواقيت معلومة يُخاطَب بها.'
:::

:::Cards cols='3'
:::Card icon='🙇' title='Devotion' kicker='The heart is present'
Look at the place of your prostration and weigh what you are saying; “Glory to my Lord, the Most High” is a sentence with a meaning you can hear. Keep the phone out of the prayer, because a present heart never needs to be hurried.
:::
:::Card icon='😌' title='Serenity' kicker='In the body'
Finish the bowing and prostration properly, settle between the two prostrations, and do not rush the salam before your body has come to rest. Standing before God is not the same as moving fast.
:::
:::Card icon='📅' title='Constancy' kicker='Day after day'
Keep the obligatory prayer in congregation for a whole week, then fix one sunnah such as the two rak‘ahs before fajr — the little that stays is more loved than a great deal that stops.
:::

:::Section title='Quick questions'
:::FAQ
:::QA q='Is tayammum valid when water is available?'
Not without a real excuse. The excuse is genuine: an illness that worsens with water, fear for a garment or a body, or severe cold with real risk. If water is there and no excuse is, use the water rather than turning to dust without a reason.
:::
:::QA q='What if I lose count of the rak‘ahs?'
If you doubt the number, build on the higher count, then say the testification and the final testification; if the shortfall becomes clear after the final testification, add the prostration of forgetfulness. Do not dwell on it — staying in the prayer is safer than getting lost in counting.
:::
:::QA q='What is the ruling on shortening prayer while travelling?'
Shortening means praying four as two: zuhr, asr and isha become two rak‘ahs, maghrib stays three, and fajr stays two. Combining zuhr with asr and maghrib with isha is allowed when there is need, and shortening continues unless you intend to stay more than four days.
:::
